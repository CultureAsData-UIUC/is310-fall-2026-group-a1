###Initial Custom Dataset
For my initial dataset, I decided to use the current top 50 songs on the Billboard Hot 100. My project is taking a look at how difficult it is to get a hit song and become a prominent artist without major label backing, so I documented the artist name, song name, and label the song was released under for each song in the top 50. This is the simplest and most efficient way to document the songs, artists and their labels.
	I decided to gather my dataset from scratch, as I felt it’s necessary to gain experience with creating your own datasets, a skill I will likely need in the future. I did not use any sort of computational tools to assist with my work, I simply used a spreadsheet to document my data. I wanted to make sure I included the entire top 50 regardless of genre or when the song was released to make sure my analysis is as fair and accurate as possible There are a couple older Halloween songs that ended up on the charts, however I decided to still include them, as I do not believe they make my analysis any less true.
	As I have always been interested in music, I knew beforehand that there are 3 major labels in the United States that the majority of popular music is released under. They are Universal, Sony and Warner music groups. I expected the majority of the songs to be released under these labels with a couple more minor labels mixed in there. However, I was surprised to find that essentially the entire top 50 was released under these 3 labels. The only exception was one song being released under HYBE, which is still a major label but for more international music, so that does not count as independent. It’s also worth noting that a lot of songs were released under multiple labels, with joint contracts between a major label and a smaller independent label. There are also many songs released under labels owned by one of the 3 major labels. To account for this, I said that if at least one of the labels a song is released under is or is owned by one of the 3 major labels, I counted it as being released by that label as they probably did most of the promotion and gave most of the funding.
	In terms of results, 24 of the songs were released under Universal, 16 were under Sony, and 9 were under Warner. I was expecting a more even split between the 3, and was expecting a couple songs to be released independently, however overall these results are not surprising to me.
	To increase my dataset in the future, I was able to find an already existing dataset online similar to mine, so I would likely combine mine with theirs. I would like to expand to the full Hot 100, as well as go back to previous weeks to get a more accurate depiction of my research question. I suspect that it would be easier to get an independent song lower on the charts, so expanding to the full 100 will likely reveal more independent releases than just focusing on the top 50. I will use web scraping on the Billboard website as a computational method. Not much will change when I scale, as my results are just based off of percentages from my data. I will attempt to automate a tracker of these percentages. The only challenge I really anticipate is formatting the data from the online dataset onto my spreadsheet in the same way my original data is formatted.
