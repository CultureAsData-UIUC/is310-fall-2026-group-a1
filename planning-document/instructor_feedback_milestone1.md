I like your idea of looking at independent versus major-label releases, and I think the focus on the past decade gives you a useful starting point. This connects directly to the group’s question of popularity and the political economy of music: who has the resources to get music produced, circulated, and recognized as successful? There is a promising project here, but I would like you to clarify the cultural question beyond counting which kind of label appears most often.

Several students have worked with Billboard charts in previous semesters, and one important starting point is understanding what those charts actually represent. How is the Hot 100 calculated, what activity does it count, and what economic interests shape that measurement? The Hot 100 and Artist 100 are also different objects, so you should explain why you would choose one or compare them. Neither should be treated as a neutral or comprehensive record of popular music. Your decade-long focus could help you investigate changes in those measurements and in how music circulates, rather than treating the period as one consistent snapshot.

My concern is that the project could become a flattened counting exercise that finds major labels have more chart entries without explaining much beyond their existing resources and influence. What would make that finding culturally interesting? You might instead ask where independent releases gain visibility, whether that differs by genre, or what chart appearances reveal about the ways independent labels operate within a consolidated marketplace. You would need to consider what falls outside the charts too: an absence from the Hot 100 does not necessarily mean an absence of cultural importance or a sustainable audience.

The other important question is how you define independent. A small label might have distribution arrangements with a major company, ownership can change, and an artist’s relationship with a label may differ between releases. Rather than forcing everything into an independent/major binary, you could preserve those relationships, the evidence supporting your classifications, and any uncertainty. I think this is a particularly useful place to bring cultural complexity into your custom dataset.

For the next milestone, please distinguish what you will create from what you will audit. What does one row in your custom dataset represent: a release, an artist, a label, or a chart appearance? You might retain the decade as your overall frame while selecting approximately 50–100 releases for close research into their label, ownership, distribution, and genre context. Explain how you selected them and what your interpretive work adds beyond the information already available in a chart dataset.

Computation should assist that creation process as well as the audit. For example, you could use Python to collect available release or label information, match records across sources, or check changing names and affiliations. You might investigate whether a music API, like Spotify or Genius, provides relevant metadata, but check access and available fields before making it central to your plan. Computationally collected information would still need verification, especially where it determines whether you classify a release as independent.

For the audit, an existing Billboard dataset could be a useful starting point. However, you would need to verify its source, coverage, missing records, and whether it represents songs, artists, or repeated weekly appearances. You could then connect it to your custom records through particular releases or artists and examine what the chart data conceals about their production and circulation. If changes in streaming or platforms such as TikTok become part of your question, explain what evidence would let you investigate that relationship rather than assuming it explains chart success.

Finally, would highly recommend starting to engage with published scholarship on music industries, independent production, or chart measurement, and explain how those sources inform your categories and methods. Overall, I think you are moving in a good direction; the next step is to identify the question that makes this more than a count, then build the custom dataset and audit around it.
